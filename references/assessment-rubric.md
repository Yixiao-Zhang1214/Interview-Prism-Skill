# Evidence-Anchored Interview Assessment Rubric

## Contents

- Scoring rules
- Shared level anchors
- Competency dimensions
- Root-cause categories
- Prohibited judgments

## Scoring Rules

- Score the evidence demonstrated in one answer or one session, not the whole person.
- Cite answer segment IDs for every scored observation.
- Use `level=null` when the material cannot support a level.
- Do not infer ability from answer length, vocabulary, confidence of tone, accent, or speaking speed alone.
- Keep real and mock observations in their source ledger.

## Shared Level Anchors

| Level | Behavioral anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Misses the question or offers assertions without a usable example, reasoning, or outcome. |
| 2 | Partially addresses the question; gives some relevant detail but leaves major evidence gaps or cannot explain personal contribution. |
| 3 | Answers the main request with a coherent example, identifiable actions, and a plausible result; important depth or trade-offs remain thin. |
| 4 | Gives a structured, specific answer with clear ownership, constraints, trade-offs, evidence, and reflection; handles relevant follow-up. |
| 5 | Meets level 4 and demonstrates transferable judgment across ambiguity, competing constraints, or pressure, with strong evidence and calibrated limitations. |

Do not award level 5 merely for completeness. It requires depth, judgment, and transfer evidence relevant to the dimension.

## Competency Dimensions

### `problem_framing` — Problem framing

- Evidence: clarifies the goal, constraints, stakeholders, success criteria, and what makes the problem difficult.
- Common gap: narrates background without identifying the decision problem.

### `reasoning_and_tradeoffs` — Reasoning and trade-offs

- Evidence: compares alternatives, states assumptions, explains prioritization, and names rejected options or risks.
- Common gap: reports an action without explaining why it was chosen.

### `execution_and_ownership` — Execution and ownership

- Evidence: distinguishes personal decisions and actions from team activity, resolves blockers, and follows through.
- Common gap: uses “we” throughout with no verifiable personal contribution.

### `data_and_outcome_evidence` — Data and outcome evidence

- Evidence: defines metrics, baseline, change, time window, attribution limits, or qualitative validation when quantitative data is unavailable.
- Common gap: says “improved significantly” without a metric or validation method.

### `structured_communication` — Structured communication

- Evidence: gives the conclusion early, groups supporting points, answers all explicit sub-questions, and stays traceable under follow-up.
- Common gap: relevant details exist but the listener must reconstruct the answer.

### `collaboration_and_influence` — Collaboration and influence

- Evidence: identifies stakeholder interests, disagreement, influence method, decisions, and relationship-preserving resolution.
- Common gap: equates scheduling a meeting with influence.

### `domain_or_technical_depth` — Domain or technical depth

- Evidence: uses correct concepts, explains mechanisms and edge cases, and connects knowledge to the role context.
- Common gap: lists terminology without explaining application.

### `reflection_and_transfer` — Reflection and transfer

- Evidence: names what changed, what would be done differently, and how the lesson applies to another situation.
- Common gap: gives a generic lesson disconnected from the example.

## Root-Cause Categories

Use exactly one primary category per gap when possible:

- `knowledge`: missing or incorrect domain knowledge.
- `reasoning`: weak assumptions, trade-offs, or causal logic.
- `question_understanding`: missed an explicit request or follow-up.
- `communication`: evidence exists but is poorly organized or too diffuse.
- `case_evidence`: lacks metrics, details, or a suitable example.
- `ownership`: personal contribution is unclear.
- `interaction_strategy`: does not clarify, confirm, or respond to interviewer cues.
- `transfer`: performs in rehearsal but lacks real-world validation or cannot generalize.

## Prohibited Judgments

Do not score or diagnose honesty, personality, mental state, emotional stability, protected characteristics, or hiring fit from vocal style. Replace unsupported claims such as “the interviewer thinks the candidate lied” with an observable alternative such as “the interviewer may continue checking project details and ownership.”
